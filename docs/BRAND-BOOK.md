# MOONSHIPS Brand Book v1.0
## Space Punk / Underground Future

**Status:** Canonical brand direction  
**Applies to:** Product UI, website and launch copy, documentation, repository, community, and social communication  
**Master tagline:** **Your Infrastructure. Your Rules.**  
**Campaign line:** **No Permission Needed.**  
**Movement line:** **Build Beyond the Default.**

---

## 1. Brand essence

MOONSHIPS is an open-source, self-hosted control plane for builders who want to operate applications, containers, databases, and servers on infrastructure they control.

The brand is not a generic cloud dashboard with a different logo. It stands for agency: the ability to choose where software runs, understand what is running, and retain operational control.

**One-line essence:** Independent infrastructure for people who build on their own terms.

**Positioning statement:** MOONSHIPS is the rebellious, self-hosted control plane for developers, indie makers, and operators who refuse to give up control of the infrastructure beneath their work.

**Brand promise:** Bring your stack together without giving away ownership of the ground it runs on.

**Enemy:** Unnecessary lock-in, opaque infrastructure, artificial limitations, needless complexity, and the idea that builders should accept somebody else's defaults.

We are not anti-cloud, anti-company, or anti-enterprise. We are pro-choice, pro-ownership, and pro-clarity.

## 2. Audience

### Primary
- Independent developers and indie hackers shipping real products.
- Small product teams that need a practical self-hosted platform.
- DevOps-minded builders operating one VPS or a growing fleet.
- Open-source users who value inspectability and control.

### Secondary
- Agencies and studios managing multiple client environments.
- Startups seeking a portable, understandable operating layer.
- Experienced operators who want a unified control plane without surrendering the underlying host.

### Audience tension
They want to ship quickly, but do not want every operational decision to become a vendor decision. They value freedom, but still need dependable security, backups, observability, and predictable behavior.

| DNA | Job | Expression | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRUNGE | Texture and authenticity | Raw edges, grain, photocopy texture, imperfect compositions | Never reduce legibility or imply instability |
| RIOT | Conviction and independence | Direct statements, anti-lock-in point of view, bold contrast | Challenge defaults, never attack users or competitors |

The result is **Space Punk / Underground Future**: orbital and cosmic motifs meet underground print culture and expressive digital color.

## 4. Message architecture

### Master tagline
**Your Infrastructure. Your Rules.**

Use on the product identity, repository overview, documentation masthead, and persistent brand placements.

### Campaign line
**No Permission Needed.**

Use in launch campaigns, social posts, hero accents, and community calls to action. It is a statement of agency, not a promise to bypass security, law, or operational safeguards.

### Movement line
**Build Beyond the Default.**

Use as a secondary headline and community-oriented sign-off.

### Product descriptor
**An open-source, self-hosted control plane for applications, containers, databases, and servers.**

Use whenever clarity matters more than attitude: SEO descriptions, README summaries, app metadata, release notes, and technical listings.

### Supporting messages
- Own your stack. Own your choices.
- One control plane. Your entire universe.
- Less platform wrestling. More building.
- Deploy your way. Operate with control.
- Your servers. Your rules.

### Proof before promise
Connect each claim to a real capability:
- Self-hosted control -> users operate the platform on their own server.
- Unified operations -> applications, containers, databases, domains, TLS, and server management.
- Resilience -> backups, deployment history, and rollback capabilities where supported.
- Fleet control -> multi-server agents and WireGuard mesh where configured.
- Openness -> source code and applicable open-source licensing.

Never claim zero cost, unlimited scale, zero lock-in, unbreakable security, or universal compatibility.

## 5. Voice and writing system

### Voice attributes
1. **Confident, not arrogant.** State what the product does; do not posture.
2. **Rebellious, not reckless.** Challenge dependency, not security discipline.
3. **Playful, not childish.** Use wit sparingly and intentionally.
4. **Technical, not intimidating.** Be precise without gatekeeping.
5. **Independent, not isolated.** Invite builders into a shared project.

### Writing rules
- Prefer short sentences and active verbs.
- Lead with user agency and concrete outcomes.
- Use contractions in informal marketing copy; use precise language in docs.
- Say "self-hosted" and explain what runs where.
- Use "control plane" for the product category.
- Use "MOONSHIPS" in all caps for the brand name.
- Keep feature names descriptive and consistent.
- Use humor only when it does not obscure an instruction or warning.
- Use inclusive language; never imply beginners are less legitimate builders.

### Avoid
- Forced slang such as "rizz", "skibidi", "no cap", or short-lived memes.
- Empty corporate phrases: "revolutionary ecosystem", "seamless digital transformation", "next-generation synergy".
- Hostile language toward users, vendors, or competing projects.
- Unqualified superlatives: "best", "unlimited", "zero maintenance", "100% secure".
- "Free" unless the exact license, hosting, and operating costs are clearly explained.

### Examples
| Generic / avoid | MOONSHIPS voice |
|---|---|
| Leverage our comprehensive platform | Run your stack. Your way. |
| Seamlessly optimize your workflow | Less platform wrestling. More building. |
| Enterprise-grade orchestration solution | Your servers. One control plane. |
| Empowering digital transformation | Build something. Own the ground beneath it. |
| Click here to configure your environment | Set up your orbit. |

## 6. Visual identity

### Art direction
**Space Punk / Underground Future.** Preserve the existing crescent moon and orbital mark as the core recognition device. Build a more expressive world around it using print textures, sharp typography, orbital diagrams, and controlled neon accents.

### Color tokens
The machine-readable source of truth is [brand-tokens.json](../branding/brand-tokens.json).

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Void | `#090B10` | Primary dark canvas |
| Carbon | `#151922` | Elevated dark surface |
| Paper | `#F4F1E8` | Warm light canvas |
| Chalk | `#FFFDF7` | High-contrast text |
| Acid | `#C7F464` | Signature action / signal |
| Orbit | `#69D8F2` | Technical highlight |
| Ultraviolet | `#9B8CFF` | Expressive secondary accent |
| Riot | `#FF6B4A` | Rare warning / campaign accent |
| Muted | `#9299A6` | Secondary text |

**Usage ratio:** 70% neutral foundation, 20% structural surfaces and typography, 10% expressive accents. Acid is the signature brand signal; do not paint every component acid green.

### Typography
- **Display:** heavy condensed or stencil-like sans for short campaign headlines. Existing self-hosted Allerta Stencil may be used for the wordmark only.
- **Interface:** system sans-serif stack for legibility and performance.
- **Technical:** system monospace for commands, identifiers, metrics, and code.
- Never use a display face for paragraphs, form labels, error messages, or dense documentation.
- Do not add a remote font dependency to the authenticated product UI.

### Graphic language
- Crescent moon and orbital paths remain the core brand mark.
- Use orbital rings, coordinates, star maps, labels, stamps, and directional arrows as supporting devices.
- Grain, halftone, photocopy noise, torn-paper edges, and sticker-like annotations belong primarily to campaign and marketing surfaces.
- Keep charts, tables, forms, and operational status indicators clean and semantically clear.
- Glitch is a rare accent, never a permanent readability effect.

### Logo rules
- Preserve original aspect ratio and clear space of at least the moon symbol's orbital stroke width.
- Use the SVG wordmark on dark backgrounds where its light wordmark is legible.
- Use the square icon for favicon, app identity, and compact placements.
- Never stretch, rotate, recolor arbitrarily, add effects that obscure the crescent, or recreate the mark with plain text.
- Do not replace existing product marks with unrelated skull, flame, or generic hacker iconography.

## 7. Product UI expression

The product interface is an operational tool, not a campaign poster. Apply the DNA through controlled details:
- Deep neutral surfaces and strong text hierarchy.
- Acid accent for primary actions and selected brand signals, with accessible contrast.
- Cyan and violet for secondary information only where semantic meaning is clear.
- Small uppercase kicker labels for section context; never replace descriptive headings.
- Clear borders, stable spacing, familiar form patterns, and predictable focus states.
- Motion is subtle, short, and nonessential. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Do not use texture behind dense tables, logs, terminal output, forms, or charts.
- Status colors retain their operational meaning. Brand color must never make healthy, warning, and failed states ambiguous.

### UX priority order
1. Correctness and safety.
2. Legibility and accessibility.
3. Information hierarchy.
4. Brand expression.

Brand styling must never change API behavior, permission checks, data handling, or existing compatibility identifiers.

## 8. Motion and sound

- Use orbital movement for decorative hero artwork only.
- Prefer 150–220 ms transitions for interface feedback.
- Avoid constant motion, flashing, parallax that impairs reading, or audio that autoplays.
- Honor reduced-motion preferences and preserve a fully understandable static state.
- No sound effects in core operational workflows.

## 9. Photography and illustration

- Prefer original diagrams, interface screenshots, abstract orbital illustrations, and builder-focused real-world photography.
- Avoid generic stock photos of people pointing at laptops.
- Do not depict illegal hacking, unauthorized access, or cybercrime as the brand's identity.
- Keep technical diagrams accurate and label conceptual artwork as conceptual.

## 12. Brand governance

- This document and `branding/brand-tokens.json` are the canonical v1 references.
- Product claims must be checked against shipped functionality before publication.
- Keep brand copy separate from compatibility identifiers such as legacy service names, paths, environment variables, executable names, cookies, and wire-protocol headers.
- Do not rename runtime contracts as a visual-brand task.
- Any new color token must include a role and accessible text/background pairing.
- Any new font must be self-hosted, licensed appropriately, and justified by measurable design value.
- Public-facing copy changes should be covered by the branding consistency gate.
- Update this document when the brand system changes; do not create competing unofficial token lists.
