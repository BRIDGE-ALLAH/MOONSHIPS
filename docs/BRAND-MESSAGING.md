# MOONSHIPS Brand Messaging
## Narrative system · v1.0

**Brand:** MOONSHIPS  
**Master tagline:** Your Infrastructure. Your Rules.  
**Campaign line:** No Permission Needed.  
**Movement line:** Build Beyond the Default.  
**Product descriptor:** An open-source, self-hosted control plane for applications, containers, databases, and servers.  
**Creative direction:** Space Punk / Underground Future

---

## 1. The story

The people building the internet should have a say in the infrastructure that runs it.

But shipping a product can pull builders into a maze of platforms, vendor-specific workflows, opaque limits, and operational decisions they do not fully control. MOONSHIPS gives them a self-hosted control plane to deploy and operate applications, containers, databases, and servers on infrastructure they choose.

This is not a story about rejecting every cloud provider or pretending infrastructure has no trade-offs. It is about making ownership and choice practical: know where your stack runs, understand how it behaves, and keep the controls within reach.

**The narrative in one sentence:** Build what matters; keep control of the ground it runs on.

## 2. Positioning

**For:** developers, indie makers, small product teams, and operators who want practical control over their infrastructure.

**Who need:** a unified way to deploy applications and manage the systems beneath them without surrendering the underlying host or being forced into one provider's operating model.

**MOONSHIPS is:** an open-source, self-hosted control plane for applications, containers, databases, and servers.

**Unlike:** fragmented workflows and opaque managed platforms, MOONSHIPS brings common deployment and operations tasks into one interface while keeping the infrastructure self-hosted.

**Because:** the platform runs on infrastructure the operator controls, and the project exposes its source and operational model.

## 5. Proof before promise

Every external claim must connect to something users can inspect or do.

| Message | Proof point | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Self-hosted control | MOONSHIPS runs on an operator-managed server | Do not imply zero maintenance |
| Deploy your way | Git deployments, Dockerfiles, images, Compose, and supported build paths | Name only supported source/build types |
| Operate the stack | Container lifecycle, logs, stats, networks, and images | Do not imply every host metric is available |
| Connect servers | Agents and WireGuard mesh where configured | Qualify multi-server setup requirements |
| Protect your work | Backup features, deployment history, and rollback where supported | Do not promise disaster-proof recovery |
| Own your choices | Open-source repository and self-hosted deployment model | Do not claim no vendor dependency in every workload |

Avoid claims such as unlimited scale, zero lock-in, zero cost, unbreakable security, effortless operations, or universal compatibility.

## 6. Voice system

MOONSHIPS voice combines four traits:

- **GRUNGE:** honest, tactile, imperfect in texture — never careless with facts.
- **RIOT:** decisive and independent — never hostile or reckless.
- **FUNKY:** energetic and memorable — never noisy or childish.
- **GEN-Z:** concise, culturally fluent, self-aware — never dependent on slang or short-lived memes.

### Writing rules
- Start with the human tension or concrete outcome.
- Use short sentences and active verbs.
- Prefer verbs over abstract nouns.
- Pair attitude with a plain-language explanation.
- Explain technical terms when the audience may not know them.
- Be candid about configuration, prerequisites, and trade-offs.
- Keep operational instructions literal and calm.

### Use
- Own your stack. Own your choices.
- Your servers. Your rules.
- Less platform wrestling. More building.
- Bring your stack into one control plane.
- Deploy where you choose. Operate with clarity.

### Avoid
- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "next-generation", and other empty superlatives.
- Forced slang, memes, or fake youth language.
- Insults aimed at users, vendors, or competitors.
- "Set it and forget it", "zero maintenance", or "100% secure".
- Claims that imply backups or rollbacks are automatic unless the specific feature is configured to do so.

## 8. Channel playbook

### Website
1. State the product category in the first viewport.
2. Name the tension: building should not mean surrendering infrastructure choices.
3. Show concrete capabilities: deploy, operate, connect, protect.
4. Qualify capabilities that depend on configuration.
5. End with a direct action: explore the repository or open the control plane.

### GitHub and README
- Product descriptor first.
- Capability list second.
- Installation and migration truth before campaign language.
- Keep compatibility notes explicit.
- Link to the Brand Book and this guide.

### Authentication and dashboard
- Authentication uses the master tagline, not a rotating campaign slogan.
- Dashboard headings describe the operational task.
- Empty states may encourage the next useful action.
- Warnings, confirmations, permission errors, and destructive actions must never be written as jokes.

### Social content pillars
1. **Build:** practical deployment workflows and supported source types.
2. **Own:** self-hosting, portability, and infrastructure choice.
3. **Operate:** logs, metrics, containers, databases, and server workflows.
4. **Protect:** backups, history, rollback, and security practices.
5. **Open:** releases, roadmap progress, contributions, and community.

### Social post pattern
- Hook: one strong, human statement.
- Proof: one concrete capability or shipped improvement.
- Action: a useful link or invitation.
- Never post a feature claim without a corresponding implementation or documented roadmap label.

### Release notes
Use this order:
1. What changed.
2. Why it matters.
3. Who is affected.
4. Compatibility or migration impact.
5. Required action, if any.

## 9. Editorial quality gate

- [ ] The reader can tell what MOONSHIPS is.

- [ ] Each capability claim is verifiable in the product or clearly labeled as planned.

- [ ] No forced slang, unsupported superlatives, or misleading security/cost claims.
- [ ] Technical instructions remain precise and calm.
